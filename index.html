    Sources & method

    Every country is dated three ways. Pick the clock that matches your argument; the map re-colours instantly and the legend and export say which clock was used.

    The three clocks

    First sovereignty
    The earliest date the state became a sovereign entity, as listed on Wikipedia. For old states this is often a founding dynasty or unification (Denmark 714, Japan 539, France 843). Fuzzy by nature.
    Last freed
    The date the state last gained or regained sovereignty from a foreign power, such as decolonisation, the end of an occupation, or secession. Internal revolutions do not count.
    Current regime
    When the political system in place today was established: a revolution, a new constitution, reunification, the end of a dictatorship. Hand-curated for 67 states with a linked source each; every other state falls back to its "last freed" date.

    Where the data comes from

    • List of modern sovereign states by date of formation (Wikipedia). Gives first sovereignty, last subordination, previous power, capital and continent for 196 states. Scraped 9 April 2026. Text is CC BY-SA 4.0.
    • Regime dates: individual Wikipedia articles on each event, linked from the country panel. Curated in add_political_date.py in the repository, reviewed 18 September 2026.
    • Taiwan is missing from the Wikipedia list and was added by hand (add_extras.py), sourced to the articles on the Republic of China and Retrocession Day.
    • Boundaries: Natural Earth 1:50m admin-0 countries, public domain.

    How the map is coloured

    • Dependent territories such as Greenland, Puerto Rico or New Caledonia take their sovereign's colour with a hatch, and are labelled as territories on hover. They are not counted as countries.
    • Disputed or unrecognised areas (Western Sahara, Somaliland, Northern Cyprus, Siachen) are hatched grey. Kosovo, Palestine and Taiwan are included as entries.
    • Ages are computed from the year only, against the current year. A country founded in 1948 reads as the same age all year.
    • Small islands get a dot so they remain visible at world scale; zoom in to see their outlines.

    Caveats

    "How old is a country" has no single right answer, which is exactly why there are three clocks. Founding dates for old states are conventions, not facts. Regime dates involve judgement: coups that replaced one junta with another were mostly not counted, and the curated list is biased toward well-documented cases. Corrections are welcome as issues or pull requests on GitHub.
